## What Dragon Grove Actually Is (and Is Not)

Schell Games classifies the project as a client project with Meta, genre Casual, Puzzle, Entertainment, platform Mixed Reality, engine Unity, audience Family and Adult. It is free to play. It is not a Roblox game, not the Steam title *Dragon's Grove*, and not *Merge Dragons*. It is also not a controller game — there is no gamepad or Touch controller play. You play with your bare hands, tracked natively by the headset.

That distinction matters more than it sounds. Petting a dragon, waving to it, cradling it, and matching leaves and flowers are all physical gestures you perform in your real room, with the game composited around you. The cozy label is not marketing garnish here; it describes the actual design intent, which is seated, low-stress play.

## The Core Loop: Match, Awaken, Bond

The advertised premise is that you match and awaken magical dragon plants, foster a grove, unlock helpful dragon abilities, and bond with dragons. The matching layer is the puzzle; the dragons are the reward layer that sits on top of it.

Because Schell Games has confirmed 3D matching puzzles with new plant types, layers, and spatial mechanics, the sensible reading is that puzzles are not flat grids. Layering and spatial mechanics generally imply that a piece's position in three-dimensional space — depth, stacking, occlusion — affects whether it can be matched. Treat that as general reasoning from the matching-puzzle premise rather than measured data; nobody outside the studio has published breakdowns of how specific mechanics resolve at higher levels.

Completing levels earns Coins. Coins are usable in the shop for dragon hats, tail decorations, and auras. Coins can also be purchased and do not expire. That is the entire documented monetization picture.

## Progression, Coins, and the Pay-to-Win Question

This is the part of the keyword that attracts the most confusion, so let us be precise about what the developer has said. Dragon boosts are power-ups that grant an extra match or an extra ability use. The game is explicitly not pay to win: all levels can be completed without power-ups. Cosmetics — hats, tail decorations, auras — are cosmetic. Dragon toys are optional unlockables that maximize dragon interaction, letting you play fetch or give a dragon a custom perch, and the developer states they have no impact on gameplay.

If you are the kind of player who wants to avoid spending, the practical approach is simple: clear levels, bank Coins from completion, and spend them on whatever cosmetic you actually want. Purchased Coins never expire, so there is no pressure to burn them before some arbitrary deadline. I would not promise that any particular spending strategy guarantees a smoother run, because nothing in the official material supports that.

## Hand Tracking, Comfort, and Setup

Native hand tracking is the whole input scheme, which means your play space and lighting genuinely matter. Mixed reality passthrough needs enough light for the headset's cameras to see your hands, and hand tracking degrades when your hands are out of the camera's view or moving too fast. If gestures like petting or cradling feel unreliable, that is usually an environment problem before it is a game problem — brighten the room, keep hands within view, and avoid sitting so close to a wall that you clip your own tracking volume.

The game is designed to be played seated, which is unusual and welcome for a puzzle title. If you have not used Quest hand tracking before, spend a few minutes in the headset's own settings to confirm tracking is stable before judging the game's input.
