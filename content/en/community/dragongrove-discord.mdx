Dragon Grove is a cozy mixed-reality puzzle game from Schell Games, built with Meta and designed to be played entirely with your bare hands. If you have seen the name floating around alongside Roblox recommendations, Steam survival games, or match-three mobile titles, that is a different thing entirely. This article sticks to what the developer has actually published on the official Dragon Grove page, plus a clear line between advertised features, what the community can reasonably infer, and what simply is not documented yet.

## What Dragon Grove Actually Is

According to the developer's official portfolio listing, Dragon Grove is a client project with Meta. Schell Games classifies it as Casual, Puzzle, and Entertainment, built in Unity for Mixed Reality. The audience is listed as Family and Adult. It is free to play, expected in spring 2027, and runs on Meta Quest 3 headsets and up.

The core loop is spatial matching. Instead of swiping on a flat grid, you match and awaken magical dragon plants with your real hands in your real room. The game uses native hand tracking only, with no controller. You pet dragons, wave to them, cradle them, and match leaves and flowers by reaching out and moving them yourself.

This is deliberately low-stress. The official materials describe seated, cozy play. There is no mention of timers, combat, or failure states that punish you, and the genre labeling supports that reading.

You can read the developer's own summary here: [Dragon Grove on schellgames.com](https://schellgames.com/portfolio/dragon-grove).

Schell Games also advertises daily streak rewards, magical charms, meeting new dragons, tracking friends' journeys, and 3D matching puzzles that introduce new plant types, layers, and spatial mechanics. Replaying groves is framed as a way to earn more stars.

Two distinctions matter here. First, dragon toys are described as optional unlockables that maximize dragon interaction, letting you play fetch or give your dragon a custom perch. They have no impact on gameplay. Second, dragon boosts are power-ups that grant an extra match or an extra ability use. Because every level is completable without them, the developer explicitly frames the game as not pay to win.

The official FAQ also states that the art, character assets, and gameplay contain no AI components.

## Hand Tracking and What It Means for Play

Native hand tracking is not a gimmick bolted onto a flat puzzler. It is the input method. There is no controller fallback described.

In practice, that shapes how you should think about playing. Reach matters more than cursor precision. Placing your hands comfortably before a session, with enough clearance in front of you, is general ergonomic reasoning rather than measured advice. Seated play is the documented posture, so a stable chair and a consistent room setup will likely serve you better than standing and leaning.

If hand tracking is new to you, the honest answer is that we cannot tell you how forgiving Dragon Grove's tracking tolerance is, because Schell Games has not published those details. You will find that out in the headset.

## How to Reason About the Puzzles

The developer describes 3D matching puzzles with new plant types, layers, and spatial mechanics across 200+ levels and 30 groves. That is the extent of the official mechanical detail.

What is not documented: level names, grove names, plant names, star thresholds, coin prices, drop rates, and the specific dragon abilities you unlock. Earlier summaries of Dragon Grove sometimes assume a set of elemental powers or themed biomes, but Schell Games has not published those. If you see a list of dragon abilities attributed to this game, it is not from the developer.

## Coins, Stars, and the Shop

Coins come from completing levels. They are spent in the shop on cosmetic dragon items: hats, tail decorations, and auras. Coins can also be purchased with real money, and the FAQ notes they do not expire, which is a meaningful detail if you buy a bundle and play slowly.

Stars come from completing and replaying groves. Replaying is advertised as a way to earn more.

What is not documented: conversion rates, bundle sizes, or price points. Do not trust any figure you see quoted. Check the in-game shop or the official page, since those are the only sources that can update.

The fairness picture is unusually clear for a free-to-play title. Since all levels are completable without power-ups, spending is about expression and convenience, not access.
