## What Dragon Grove Actually Is

Dragon Grove is a casual puzzle and entertainment experience built in Unity for mixed reality. It is a seated, low-stress game with a family-and-adult audience in mind. That framing matters more than it sounds: "cozy" here is a design goal, not marketing garnish. The game is meant to be played sitting down, at your own pace, without the pressure that usually comes with puzzle timers or competitive ladders.

The interaction model is the headline feature. You play with your hands, not a controller. You pet dragons, wave to them, cradle them, and match leaves and flowers by physically reaching out and manipulating objects in your real room. No controller mapping means no button prompts to memorize, but it does mean your play space and lighting conditions will affect how smoothly hand tracking behaves.

It is worth restating the identity clearly, because the keyword confusion is genuine. This is not a Roblox game. It is not the Steam title Dragon's Grove from Frozen Flame. It is not Merge Dragons. Treat any article that blends those together as unreliable on the basics.

## What Mixed Reality Changes About Puzzle Play

Matching puzzles are a familiar genre, and the core loop here is recognizable: match leaves and flowers, complete levels, earn Coins, awaken magical dragon plants, and grow your grove. What mixed reality changes is how you perform the match.

In a flat-screen match-three, you swipe. Here, you physically reach into a spatial arrangement that sits in your room. That has practical consequences worth reasoning about, even though no measured data exists yet. Because you are matching in three dimensions rather than on a grid, the limiting factor is likely to be depth perception and reach, not timing. That suggests a few general habits, framed as reasoning from the premise rather than tested advice:

- Sit where your hands have clear, unobstructed room to move. Hand tracking degrades when your hands overlap clutter or leave the sensor's comfortable zone.
- Play in steady, even lighting. Hand tracking is camera-based, so harsh backlighting or a dark room works against you.
- Scan the whole arrangement before reaching. A 3D layout hides matches behind other objects in a way a flat board cannot.
- Slow down on purpose. The game is designed to be low-stress; there is no documented reward for speed, and rushing increases mis-tracking.

Again, these are inferences from how mixed-reality hand tracking and spatial puzzles work, not documented mechanics or measured results.

## Coins, Cosmetics, and the Honest Monetization Picture

Free-to-play games invite suspicion, so it is worth being precise. Coins are earned by completing levels. They can also be purchased, and they do not expire. They are spent in the shop on dragon hats, tail decorations, and auras. Those are cosmetic. Dragon toys are optional unlockables that expand interaction, letting you play fetch or give a dragon a custom perch, and they explicitly do not affect gameplay. Dragon boosts are power-ups granting an extra match or ability use, and the game can be finished without them.

What is not documented: coin prices, bundle structures, whether any cosmetics are time-limited, and whether a battle pass or subscription exists. As of the official FAQ, none of that is confirmed. Do not trust a site quoting specific coin costs or a season schedule. The FAQ also states that the art, character assets, and gameplay contain no AI components, which is a detail worth knowing given how often that question comes up.
