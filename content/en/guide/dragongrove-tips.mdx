If you have searched for "Dragon Grove" lately, you have probably landed on a Roblox map, a Steam survival game, or a match-three mobile title. None of those are the Dragon Grove this article covers. The Dragon Grove verified here is the mixed-reality game from Schell Games, built as a client project with Meta and listed on the studio's own portfolio page at https://schellgames.com/portfolio/dragon-grove. It is a cozy 3D spatial puzzle game designed exclusively for Mixed Reality, and it is played entirely with native hand tracking on Meta Quest 3 headsets and up. This article sticks to what the developer has published, flags what comes from third-party guides, and states plainly where the record is silent.

## The identity check: which Dragon Grove you are looking at

Before anything else, sort out the name collision. The developer pages are explicit that this is a first-party Schell Games project made with Meta, built in Unity, tagged as casual, puzzle, and entertainment, aimed at family and adult players. It is not a Roblox experience, it is not Dragon's Grove from Frozen Flame on Steam, and it is not Merge Dragons. If a page you are reading talks about Robux, survival crafting, or stamina timers, you are on the wrong Dragon Grove.

The game is free to play, currently expected in spring 2027, and targeted at Meta Quest 3 and newer headsets. No controller is used at all. You pet, wave to, and cradle dragons with your hands, and you match leaves and flowers by physically moving them. That physicality is the whole point of the design: it is seated, cozy, low-stress play rather than an action game.

## What the developer actually advertises

The official materials describe a fairly specific loop. You match and awaken magical dragon plants, build up your grove, unlock helpful dragon abilities, and bond with dragons as you go. At launch that loop spans 200+ levels across 30 unique groves, with more content promised through an official roadmap and patch notes rather than announced in advance.

Everyone starts with Pip, the signature green dragon. Eighteen dragons are available at launch, and the developer states all of them are unlockable through progression. Completing levels earns Coins, which you spend in the shop on dragon hats, tail decorations, and auras. Coins can also be purchased, and purchased Coins do not expire.

The official page also advertises daily streak rewards, magical charms, meeting new dragons, tracking friends' journeys, 3D matching puzzles that introduce new plant types, layers, and spatial mechanics, and replaying groves to earn more stars.

Two points get misread often. Dragon toys are optional unlockables that maximise dragon interaction — playing fetch or giving a dragon a custom perch — and the developer says they have no impact on gameplay. Dragon boosts are power-ups, and the game is explicitly not pay to win: every level can be completed without power-ups. That is a design claim from the studio, not a guarantee about your personal experience, but it is the stated intent.

## Hand tracking, comfort, and who this is for

Because Dragon Grove runs on native hand tracking, the practical questions are about your play space rather than your reflexes. It is seated and cozy by design, which suits players who want a low-intensity session. The audience is listed as family and adult, and nothing in the verified description suggests twitch timing or score pressure.

The one common-sense caveat: hand tracking quality depends on lighting, hand visibility, and how you sit relative to the headset's sensors. That is a property of the hardware, not a documented Dragon Grove mechanic, so treat it as general reasoning rather than a spec.

What separates Dragon Grove from a flat match-three game is spatial matching. The developer describes 3D matching puzzles with new plant types, layers, and spatial mechanics, which implies puzzles that use depth and arrangement rather than a single grid. Beyond that description, the official sources do not document specific puzzle rules, grove themes, or how individual mechanics evolve across the 30 groves. If you want those details, the honest answer is that they are not published yet.

Likewise, dragon abilities are described as "helpful" and unlockable, but the official material does not enumerate what any specific dragon's ability does. Third-party guides sometimes speculate about abilities and grove themes; those are secondary observations, not developer documentation, and should be treated accordingly. ## Practical reasoning for new players

None of the following is measured data. It is reasoning from the matching-puzzle premise plus the verified feature list.
