Dragon Grove is easy to confuse with something it is not. Search the name and you will surface a Roblox experience, a Steam survival game with a similar title, and a long-running mobile match-three series. None of those are this project. Dragon Grove, as documented on the official Schell Games portfolio page at https://schellgames.com/portfolio/dragon-grove, is a mixed-reality puzzle game built with Meta for Meta Quest 3 headsets and up. It is free to play, expected in spring 2027, and designed from the ground up around native hand tracking rather than controllers.

That last detail shapes everything. This is not a port with optional hand gestures bolted on. It is a seated, low-stress game you play by reaching into your own room.

## What Dragon Grove Actually Is

According to Schell Games, Dragon Grove is a client project with Meta. The genre is listed as Casual, Puzzle, and Entertainment, built in Unity for Mixed Reality, aimed at a family and adult audience. You match and awaken magical dragon plants, tend a grove, unlock helpful dragon abilities, and bond with the dragons themselves.

Play happens entirely through native hand tracking. No controller is required. The advertised interactions include petting dragons, waving to them, cradling them, and matching leaves and flowers with your hands directly. The game is explicitly described as seated, cozy, and low-stress, which is a meaningful design commitment for a headset game. Many mixed-reality titles assume you will stand and move; this one does not.

The launch scope is documented: 200+ levels across 30 unique groves, with more content promised through an official roadmap and patch notes. At launch there are 18 dragons, and all of them are unlockable through progression. Everyone begins with Pip, the signature green dragon.

## How the Matching Premise Shapes Strategy

General reasoning, not measured data: in any matching game with spatial layers, the hardest decisions come from what is buried. If puzzles involve depth and multiple plant types, the practical habits are to clear the most obstructive layer first rather than the most visible one, to avoid spending a boost on a board you have not read yet, and to hold power-ups for boards where you can already see the solution but cannot reach it.

Because all levels are stated to be completable without power-ups, boosts function as a time saver rather than a gate. That also means there is no documented reason to buy Coins for progression. Coins buy cosmetic hats, tail decorations, and auras, which is a purely expressive layer. The dragon toys are described as maximizing interaction, with play fetch and custom perches mentioned, and the official material says they have no impact on gameplay. If you want more dragon time rather than more stars, that is the category to look at.

## Why Hand Tracking Matters Here

Because there is no controller path, physical setup affects the experience more than in a typical VR game. Hand tracking depends on your headset seeing your hands, so a seated position with clear space in front of you and reasonable lighting is the baseline. The game is designed for seated play, so there is no reason to stand.

The gesture list is documented at a high level: pet, wave to, and cradle dragons, and match leaves and flowers with your hands. Everything else about how those gestures feel in practice will only be verifiable by playing. Community impressions will be useful here, but they are impressions, not specifications.

## Who This Is For and Who It Is Not

Dragon Grove targets family and adult players who want something calm. Daily streak rewards, friend journey tracking, and star-based replay suggest a game built around gentle return visits rather than long sessions. The "replay groves to earn more stars" feature tells you the design expects you to revisit content rather than race through it.

It is not a competitive game, not a Roblox title, and not related to any other product sharing the name. If you are looking for the Steam survival game, you are in the wrong place. If you are looking for a comfortable mixed-reality puzzle you can play sitting down with your hands, this is the intended audience.

## FAQ

Yes. The official sources list it as free to play. Coins can be purchased, but the game is not pay to win, and all levels can be completed without power-ups.

**What headset do I need?**
Meta Quest 3 or higher. The game is built specifically for Mixed Reality with native hand tracking, and no controller is used.

**When does it come out?**
Spring 2027 is the expected window. No more specific date is documented.

**How many dragons are there?**
18 at launch, all unlockable through progression. You start with Pip.

**Does it use AI to generate art or gameplay?**
