Search "Dragongrove" and you will find at least four unrelated things fighting for the same word. There is a Roblox experience, a Steam survival game called Dragon's Grove from Frozen Flame, and a long-running mobile match-3 series with a similar name. None of them are the subject of this article. Dragon Grove, the game made by Schell Games in collaboration with Meta, is a cozy mixed-reality puzzle title built exclusively for mixed reality on Meta Quest 3 headsets and up — and the distinctions matter if you are trying to figure out what you would actually be playing.

The official product page at [schellgames.com/portfolio/dragon-grove](https://schellgames.com/portfolio/dragon-grove) lays out the pitch: match and awaken magical dragon plants, foster a grove, unlock helpful dragon abilities, and bond with dragons. It is free to play, expected in spring 2027, and played entirely with native hand tracking — no controller at all.

## What Dragon Grove Is, in Plain Terms

This is a seated, low-stress, family-friendly game. You use your real hands in your real room: pet a dragon, wave at it, cradle it, and match leaves and flowers by physically moving them. Because it runs in mixed reality rather than fully virtual reality, your physical space stays part of the experience rather than being replaced by it.

Schell Games is an established studio, and this is a client project with Meta rather than an independent release. The engine is Unity. The advertised genre tags are casual, puzzle, and entertainment, with an audience described as family and adult.

One detail worth repeating because it is frequently assumed otherwise: the official FAQ states that the art, character assets, and gameplay contain no AI components. That is a direct developer statement, not an inference.

## The Monetization Question, Answered Carefully

This is where a lot of free-to-play coverage goes wrong, so be precise. The official FAQ states the game is not pay to win because all levels can be completed without power-ups. Coins buy cosmetics — hats, tail decorations, auras — and dragon toys, which are optional unlockables with no gameplay impact.

What is not documented: coin prices for any specific item, the rate at which coins accumulate per level, or whether any dragon requires payment. The sources say all 18 dragons are unlockable through progression, which strongly implies none are paywalled, but no price list exists publicly. If you want certainty, check the in-game shop at launch, where prices are listed before you spend anything.

## Applying Matching-Puzzle Logic to Dragon Grove

No third-party guide has measured Dragon Grove's difficulty curve, and this article will not pretend otherwise. What follows is general reasoning from the matching-puzzle premise, not tested data.

In most match-based puzzle games, progress stalls for one of three reasons: a board layout that buries required pieces under immovable obstacles, a limited move count, or a mechanic introduced faster than the player can internalize it. Dragon Grove's advertised layers and spatial mechanics suggest the third category will matter more than usual, because a mechanic you have to perform with your hands in three dimensions takes longer to learn than a tap on a flat screen.

The practical implication is that hand-tracking precision, not puzzle logic, may be the real skill ceiling early on. Reaching, pinching, and placing objects in mid-air rewards steady movement over fast movement. If a level feels impossible, the problem may be motor control rather than pattern recognition.

Dragon boosts exist as that safety valve — an extra match or an extra ability use. Since levels are completable without them, treating boosts as a resource to save rather than spend is the conservative approach. That is reasoning, not a strategy the developer has endorsed.

Replaying groves to earn more stars is advertised, which means a level you barely cleared is worth returning to. Whether star thresholds change on replay is not documented.

## FAQ

**Is Dragon Grove a Roblox game?**
No. It is a mixed-reality game from Schell Games made with Meta, built for Meta Quest 3 and up.

**Do I need controllers?**
No. The game uses native hand tracking only. You pet, wave to, and cradle dragons with your hands.

**Is it pay to win?**
The official FAQ says no, because every level can be completed without power-ups. Coins go toward cosmetics and optional toys.

**How many dragons are there?**
18 at launch, all unlockable through progression. Everyone starts with Pip.

**When does it come out?**
Expected spring 2027. No specific date has been announced.

**Do I need a Meta Quest 3 specifically?**
The official materials say Quest 3 headsets and up, which excludes older Quest 2 hardware.

For anything not covered here, the developer's own page is the only reliable source — and it will be updated before anyone else's guide is.
