If you have searched for "Dragon Grove" lately, you have probably run into several different things wearing a similar name: a Roblox experience, a Steam survival game called Dragon's Grove, and the mobile match-3 hit Merge Dragons. None of those are the subject of this article. The Dragon Grove covered here is the mixed-reality game from Schell Games, made in collaboration with Meta, and listed on the studio's official portfolio at [schellgames.com/portfolio/dragon-grove](https://schellgames.com/portfolio/dragon-grove). It is a casual spatial puzzle game for Meta Quest 3 headsets and up, played entirely with native hand tracking.

Because the game has not launched yet — Schell Games lists a spring 2027 window — almost everything worth knowing right now comes from the developer's own materials rather than hands-on play. This article separates what the studio has officially advertised, what early community discussion suggests, and what simply is not documented anywhere yet.

## What Dragon Grove Actually Is

According to the official portfolio page, Dragon Grove is a client project with Meta in the Casual, Puzzle, and Entertainment genres, built in Unity and designed exclusively for Mixed Reality. The audience is described as Family and Adult. It is free to play and expected in spring 2027 on Meta Quest 3 headsets and up.

The official description frames the game as a cozy, low-stress experience played from a seated position. You match and awaken magical dragon plants, build up your grove, unlock helpful dragon abilities, and bond with individual dragons. The entire interface runs on native hand tracking — no controller. Official materials describe petting dragons, waving to them, cradling them, and matching leaves and flowers with your own hands.

That last point matters for anyone comparing this to a controller-based VR puzzler. Hand tracking is not an optional input mode here; it is the input mode. If you have used Quest 3 hand tracking before, you already know what to expect from the tracking quality, and the game is designed around those strengths rather than around thumbsticks.

## The Puzzle Structure and Grove Progression

Schell Games advertises 200+ levels at launch spread across 30 unique groves, with more content to be announced in an official roadmap or patch notes. The official page lists daily streak rewards, magical charms, 3D matching puzzles with new plant types, new layers, and spatial mechanics, plus the ability to replay groves to earn more stars. A star system implies each level has a performance ceiling worth returning for, though the exact star thresholds are not documented publicly.

The feature list also mentions meeting new dragons and tracking friends' journeys. Everything beyond those bullet points — specific grove names, level names, plant species, and the themes of individual groves — is not documented. Do not trust any list you see online claiming otherwise; that information is not available from Schell Games at the time of writing.

## Dragons, Coins, Hats, and the Pay-to-Win Question

Everyone starts with Pip, the signature green dragon. The verified total is 18 dragons at launch, and all of them are unlockable through progression rather than purchase. Completing levels earns Coins, which can be spent in the shop on dragon hats, tail decorations, and auras. Coins can also be purchased with real money and do not expire.

This is where a lot of players get nervous, so it is worth being precise. The official FAQ states the game is not pay to win, and that all levels can be completed without power-ups. Dragon boosts are power-ups that grant an extra match or an extra ability use, and dragon toys are optional unlockables that maximize dragon interaction — fetch, a custom perch — with no impact on gameplay.

| Item type | What it does | Gameplay impact |
|---|---|---|
| Dragon hats, tail decorations, auras | Cosmetic shop items bought with Coins | None |
| Dragon boosts | Power-ups granting an extra match or ability use | Optional; levels beatable without them |
| Dragon toys | Play fetch, give a custom perch | None per official FAQ |

If you want a relaxed game you can finish without spending, the official documentation supports that. If you want to buy Coins to decorate your dragons, that is also an option. Either way, the free-to-play structure sounds closer to a cosmetic shop than a progression gate.

## Hand Tracking and Mixed Reality: Practical Considerations

Since Dragon Grove runs on hand tracking only, the practical setup advice is straightforward and comes from general reasoning about the technology rather than measured data. Good lighting helps hand tracking on Quest headsets. Keeping your hands within the headset's tracking field of view, rather than resting them at your sides, tends to produce steadier input. Because the game is seated and cozy by design, you are not expected to wave your arms around a room.

The mixed-reality angle also explains the matching mechanic. Placing a 3D puzzle layer into your physical space means depth cues come from your actual room, not only from virtual rendering. When a puzzle adds a new layer or a spatial mechanic, that added dimension is the whole point — you are matching in three dimensions, not on a flat board.

If you are unsure whether your headset qualifies, the official page and FAQ are the authoritative source. Meta Quest 3 and above is what Schell Games states.
