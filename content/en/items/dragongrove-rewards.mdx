## What Dragon Grove Actually Is (and What It Is Not)

Dragon Grove is a casual puzzle and entertainment title built in Unity for Mixed Reality. It is a client project between Schell Games and Meta, free to play, and expected in spring 2027 on Quest 3 and newer headsets. It is designed to be seated, low-stress, and cozy, which means no timers forcing panic and no requirement to stand or clear a room-scale play space.

What makes it structurally unusual is the input model: it runs entirely on native hand tracking, with no controller required. You pet dragons, wave to them, cradle them, and match leaves and flowers using your actual hands. That is not a gimmick bolted onto a flat game. The matching happens in 3D space, which is why the official materials describe 3D matching puzzles with new plant types, layers, and spatial mechanics rather than a 2D grid.

To be explicit about identity: this is not a Roblox game, not the Steam game Dragon's Grove from Frozen Flame, and not Merge Dragons. If a page you land on mentions Robux, survival crafting, or merging eggs on a phone, you are on the wrong page.

## Advertised Features You Can Rely On

The developer-published details are concrete and worth treating as the baseline. At launch, the game is expected to include:

- 200+ levels across 30 unique groves, with more content to be announced through the official roadmap and patch notes.
- 18 dragons total, all unlockable through progression. Everyone begins with Pip, the signature green dragon.
- Coins earned by completing levels, spendable in a shop on dragon hats, tail decorations, and auras. Coins can also be purchased and do not expire.
- Daily streak rewards, magical charms, meeting new dragons, tracking friends' journeys, and replaying groves to earn more stars.
- Dragon toys as optional unlockables that maximize interaction, such as playing fetch or giving your dragon a custom perch.
- Dragon boosts as power-ups that grant an extra match or ability use.

| Advertised feature | Practical meaning |

Two clarifications from the official FAQ matter a lot. First, the game is not pay to win: all levels can be completed without power-ups. Second, the art, character assets, and gameplay contain no AI components. Both statements come from Schell Games directly, not from speculation.

## How to Reason About the Puzzles Before Launch

No measured difficulty data exists, so any strategy talk has to be framed as general reasoning from the matching-puzzle premise, not tested fact.

The official description emphasizes 3D matching with layers and spatial mechanics. That implies the puzzle is not purely about finding identical icons. It is likely about position, depth, and order of operations. A few sensible inferences follow. If layers exist, clearing a top layer may expose or change what is matchable below, which rewards looking before grabbing rather than reacting. If plant types interact, matching one type may enable or block another, so a short scan before your first move probably beats speed. And since levels can be replayed for more stars, a level you barely clear is not a dead end; it is a candidate for a cleaner attempt later.

What is genuinely useful to practice now is hand tracking fluency. Pinching, cradling, and waving accurately with bare hands is a physical skill. Seated sessions with good lighting and hands unobstructed tend to help tracking reliability on Quest hardware generally. That is a hardware observation, not a Dragon Grove measurement.

**Is Dragon Grove a Roblox game?** No. It is a Mixed Reality game from Schell Games made with Meta, for Meta Quest 3 and up.

**Do I need controllers?** No. It is played entirely with native hand tracking.

**Is it pay to win?** No, according to the official FAQ. All levels can be completed without power-ups, and all 18 dragons are unlockable through progression.
