If you have searched for "Dragon Grove" and landed on pages about a Roblox experience, a Steam survival game called Dragon's Grove from Frozen Flame, or the mobile puzzler Merge Dragons, you are in the wrong grove. The Dragon Grove covered here is a mixed-reality game from Schell Games, built as a client project with Meta. It is a seated, low-stress 3D spatial puzzle game played with native hand tracking on Meta Quest 3 headsets and up, and it is free to play with an expected launch in spring 2027. You can read the official overview at [Schell Games' Dragon Grove page](https://schellgames.com/portfolio/dragon-grove).

## What Dragon Grove Actually Is

The official project description places Dragon Grove in the casual puzzle and entertainment category, built in Unity for Mixed Reality, aimed at family and adult audiences. That combination matters. "Mixed reality" here is not a marketing synonym for virtual reality. The game is designed exclusively for MR, which means it uses passthrough so your real room stays visible while puzzle elements and dragons appear inside it.

Play is entirely hands-on, in the literal sense. There is no controller scheme to learn. You pet dragons, wave to them, cradle them, and match leaves and flowers using your actual hands. That design choice shapes everything else: the game is seated, cozy, and low-stress by intent rather than as an afterthought.

One clarification worth stating plainly, because it circulates in search results: the official FAQ states that the art, character assets, and gameplay contain no AI components. If you have seen claims otherwise, they are not supported by the developer's own documentation.

The official page also advertises daily streak rewards, magical charms, opportunities to meet new dragons, the ability to track friends' journeys, 3D matching puzzles that introduce new plant types, layers, and spatial mechanics, and the option to replay groves to earn more stars.

Coins are the progression currency. Completing levels earns them, and they can be spent in the shop on dragon hats, tail decorations, and auras. Coins can also be purchased, and purchased coins do not expire. Two categories sit alongside coins: dragon toys, which are optional unlockables that maximize dragon interaction, such as playing fetch or giving your dragon a custom perch, and which have no impact on gameplay; and dragon boosts, which are power-ups granting an extra match or an extra ability use.

On the pay-to-win question, the official position is explicit: all levels can be completed without power-ups. That is the developer's stated design, not a player's verdict, and you can verify it yourself by clearing content without spending.

## Why Hand Tracking Changes the Feel

Most puzzle games ask you to move a cursor. Dragon Grove asks you to use your hands in space, which is why the cozy framing is credible rather than decorative. Petting a dragon and cradling it are physical gestures, not menu selections. The trade-off is that hand tracking demands decent lighting and a clear play space, which is a reasonable expectation to set before launch rather than a documented requirement.

The seated, low-stress design also suggests sessions do not require standing stamina. For players who want something calm rather than competitive, that positioning is the point. Community observations on how hand tracking performs across different room setups are not yet meaningful, since the game is not out at the time of writing. Anyone claiming detailed performance impressions for a spring 2027 release is ahead of the available information.
