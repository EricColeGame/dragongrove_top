Dragon Grove is easy to mix up with something else. Search the name and you will find a Roblox experience, a Steam survival game called Dragon's Grove, and a match-three mobile title with a similar name. None of those are this game. Dragon Grove, as documented on the [official Schell Games portfolio page](https://schellgames.com/portfolio/dragon-grove), is a cozy 3D spatial puzzle game built exclusively for Mixed Reality on Meta Quest 3 headsets and up. It is a client project between Schell Games and Meta, made in Unity, and it is free to play with an expected launch in spring 2027. This article sticks to what the developer has actually confirmed and flags clearly where information stops.

## What Is Dragon Grove?

Dragon Grove is a casual, family-friendly mixed-reality game where you match and awaken magical dragon plants, grow a personal grove, and bond with dragons. It is designed for seated, low-stress play, which matters more than it sounds. A "cozy" label is often marketing, but here it is backed by concrete design choices: seated orientation, no controller requirement, and no combat or fail states described anywhere in the official materials.

The mixed-reality angle is the defining feature. You are not transported to a fantasy world. The game places its grove, plants, and dragons into your actual room using the Quest 3's passthrough and spatial understanding. That is why it is billed as Mixed Reality rather than plain VR, and why the game requires Quest 3 or newer hardware instead of older headsets.

## Hand Tracking Is the Whole Interface

Dragon Grove is played entirely with native hand tracking. There is no controller input. The official description lists the actions directly: pet dragons, wave to them, cradle them, and match leaves and flowers using your hands.

This is worth pausing on, because hand tracking quality varies across apps. The developer is not offering a controller fallback, which signals confidence in the interaction model but also means your play experience depends on your room's lighting and your hand-tracking comfort. If you have never used native hand tracking on Quest 3, expect a short adjustment period before gestures feel natural. Practically speaking, a well-lit space and keeping your hands within the headset's tracking volume will matter more here than in controller-based games.

## Advertised Features and Progression

The verified feature set is specific, so it is worth listing what is confirmed versus what players often assume.

| Confirmed at launch | Detail |
|---|---|
| Levels | 200+ across 30 unique groves |
| Dragons | 18 total, all unlockable through progression; Pip is the starter |
| Currency | Coins from completing levels; buyable Coins do not expire |
| Cosmetics | Dragon hats, tail decorations, auras |
| Optional unlocks | Dragon toys — fetch, custom perch |
| Power-ups | Dragon boosts granting an extra match or ability use |

A few clarifications that matter. Dragon toys maximize dragon interaction but have no gameplay impact. Dragon boosts are power-ups, and the game is explicitly described as not pay to win: every level can be completed without them. Coins spent on cosmetics are the main monetization, alongside optional Coin purchases.

The official page also advertises daily streak rewards, magical charms, meeting new dragons, tracking friends' journeys, and 3D matching puzzles that introduce new plant types, layers, and spatial mechanics. Replaying groves lets you earn more stars.

Here is where you should be skeptical of any guide you read: Schell Games has not documented specific grove names, plant types, dragon abilities, or a full dragon roster beyond Pip. If a site lists "all 18 dragons" with names and stats, that content is not from developer documentation. Dragon abilities are mentioned as an advertised feature, but their actual mechanics are not described in the sources reviewed. You can verify the real list in game after launch, or watch for the official roadmap and patch notes the developer says will follow.

## Reasoning About the Puzzles

No third-party guide I would treat as reliable has measured Dragon Grove's difficulty curve, because the game has not launched. So any strategy advice here is general reasoning from the matching-puzzle premise, not tested data.

The official description says puzzles involve matching leaves and flowers with your hands, with new plant types, layers, and spatial mechanics appearing over time. Layers and spatial mechanics suggest puzzles may involve vertical arrangement or depth rather than a flat grid — meaning where you place your hands in 3D space could matter, not just which items you select. If that is accurate, scanning the full grove from multiple angles before matching is a reasonable habit, since objects behind others may be easy to miss.

Because the game is explicitly low-stress and completable without power-ups, the practical advice is simple: treat dragon boosts as conveniences for a stuck moment, not requirements. Save Coins for cosmetics you actually want, since Coins do not expire. And replay groves for extra stars rather than assuming you must move forward immediately.

## FAQ

**Is Dragon Grove a Roblox game?**
No. It is a Meta Quest 3 Mixed Reality game by Schell Games, made with Meta.

**Do I need controllers?**
No. It uses native hand tracking only.

**Which headsets work?**
Meta Quest 3 and up, per the official description.

**How much does it cost?**
It is free to play. Cosmetics and Coins can be purchased; the game is stated not to be pay to win.

**When does it come out?**
Expected spring 2027. No specific day has been announced.

**Is there AI-generated content?**
The official FAQ says the art, character assets, and gameplay contain no AI components.
