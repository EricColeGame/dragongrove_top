If you searched for "Dragon Grove" and landed on a page about a Roblox world, a Steam survival game, or a match-three mobile title, you are in the wrong grove. Dragon Grove is a specific, documented project: a cozy 3D spatial puzzle game built by Schell Games in collaboration with Meta, designed exclusively for Mixed Reality and played with native hand tracking on Meta Quest 3 headsets and newer. Schell Games' own portfolio page for the project describes it as a client project with Meta in the Casual, Puzzle, and Entertainment genres, built in Unity, aimed at family and adult players. It is free to play, expected in spring 2027, and it is seated, low-stress, and controller-free by design.

That combination — mixed reality, native hand tracking, no controllers, no combat — is unusual enough that most of the confusion around the name comes from the title, not the game. ## What Dragon Grove Actually Is

Dragon Grove is a spatial matching puzzle game you play in your real room. According to the official description on the [Dragon Grove project page](https://schellgames.com/portfolio/dragon-grove), you use your bare hands to match and awaken magical dragon plants, build up a grove, unlock helpful dragon abilities, and bond with dragons. You pet, wave to, and cradle dragons with your hands. Matching is done by physically handling leaves and flowers in 3D space rather than tapping a flat screen.

The official FAQ confirms a few identity points that matter for anyone filtering search results:

- It is played entirely with native hand tracking — no controller.
- It runs on Meta Quest 3 headsets and up.
- It is free to play.
- The art, character assets, and gameplay contain no AI components, per the FAQ.
- It is not a Roblox experience, not Dragon's Grove from Frozen Flame, and not Merge Dragons.

The name is the only thing those products share.

## Advertised Features, Drawn From Official Sources

Schell Games advertises a specific feature set. These are the developer's own claims, not community measurements:

- **200+ levels at launch across 30 unique groves**, with more content promised through an official roadmap and/or patch notes.
- **18 dragons at launch**, all unlockable through progression. Everyone starts with Pip, the signature green dragon.
- **Coins** earned by completing levels, spendable in a shop on dragon hats, tail decorations, and auras. Coins can also be purchased and do not expire.
- **Dragon toys** as optional unlockables that maximize interaction — playing fetch, giving your dragon a custom perch. The official FAQ states toys have no impact on gameplay.
- **Dragon boosts** as power-ups granting an extra match or an extra ability use. Schell Games states the game is not pay to win and that all levels can be completed without power-ups.
- **Daily streak rewards, magical charms, meeting new dragons, tracking friends' journeys**, and replaying groves to earn more stars.
- **3D matching puzzles** that introduce new plant types, layers, and spatial mechanics over time.

## Reasoning About the Puzzle, Without Pretending to Have Data

Because Dragon Grove is fundamentally a matching puzzle, some general reasoning applies — clearly labeled as inference from the premise, not measured playtesting.

Spatial matching puzzles in mixed reality tend to reward **scanning the whole play space before committing**. When you match with your hands rather than a cursor, the bottleneck is usually reach and precision, not reaction speed. That suggests prioritizing matches you can physically reach comfortably from a seated position, and rotating your attention as the board empties.

Cozy, low-stress design usually means the fail state is soft. Schell Games explicitly says every level is completable without power-ups, which implies boosts are convenience, not a gate. If you hit a wall, the documented path forward is replaying groves for more stars and progressing your dragons — not purchasing your way past it.

Finally, dragon toys are stated to have zero gameplay impact. If your goal is efficiency, they are cosmetic-adjacent. If your goal is the cozy part of "cozy puzzle," they are the point.

## Mixed Reality, Hand Tracking, and What That Means in Practice

Playing entirely with native hand tracking means your hands are the input device. Based on how hand tracking works on Quest hardware generally — and this is general platform reasoning, not a Dragon Grove-specific claim — expect good results from deliberate, slower motions and clear hand visibility in your play area. Bright, uncluttered rooms and sleeves that don't bunch over your wrists tend to help tracking on any hand-tracked title.

Because the game is seated and low-stress, session length is your call. Nothing in the official materials describes timers or urgency mechanics, and the FAQ frames the experience around bonding and building rather than pressure.
