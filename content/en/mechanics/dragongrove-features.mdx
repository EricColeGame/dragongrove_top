Dragon Grove is easy to confuse with something it is not. Search the name and you will find a Roblox experience, a Steam survival game, and a mobile match-three title that all borrow similar words. The Dragon Grove discussed here is a specific project: a cozy mixed-reality puzzle game from Schell Games, built as a client project with Meta and designed to be played with your bare hands on a Meta Quest 3 or newer headset. If you have seen a trailer of a small green dragon sitting on a real table in someone's living room, that is this game.

This article sticks to what Schell Games has actually published about Dragon Grove and clearly flags the rest. Where details are not documented, it says so rather than filling the gaps with guesses.

## What Dragon Grove Actually Is

According to the official Dragon Grove page, the game is a mixed-reality title in the casual puzzle and entertainment genre, built in Unity and played on Meta Quest 3 headsets and up. It is a client project with Meta. It is free to play, and Schell Games has stated it is expected in spring 2027.

The design goals are stated plainly: seated, cozy, low-stress play. You do not need to stand, clear a room, or swing your arms. The entire experience runs on native hand tracking with no controller, which shapes both the puzzles and the dragon interaction.

That combination is the core of the pitch. Dragon Grove is not a virtual world you travel to. It is a set of magical plants and creatures that appear in your actual room, on your actual furniture, at a scale that makes reaching out and touching them feel natural.

## The Hand-Tracking Premise and How It Shapes Play

Because there is no controller, every interaction is a hand gesture. Schell Games describes petting dragons, waving to them, cradling them, and matching leaves and flowers directly with your hands. That sounds like a gimmick until you think about what it changes.

In a controller-based matching game, precision comes from a cursor. In Dragon Grove, precision comes from your fingers, which means the puzzle design has to be forgiving enough to feel good and spatial enough to justify mixed reality at all. The official page advertises "3D matching puzzles with new plant types, layers and spatial mechanics," which suggests puzzles that use depth and placement rather than only color or shape matching on a flat board.

A practical note for anyone planning to play: hand tracking works best in reasonable lighting and with your hands within the headset's camera view. That is general mixed-reality reasoning, not a Dragon Grove specification, but it is the kind of thing that determines whether a session feels smooth or fiddly.

## Dragons, Groves, and Progression

The verified progression structure is straightforward. Dragon Grove ships with 200+ levels across 30 unique groves at launch, with more content to be announced through an official roadmap or patch notes. Everyone begins with Pip, the signature green dragon. There are 18 dragons total at launch, and Schell Games states all of them are unlockable through normal progression.

Completing levels earns Coins. Coins are spent in the shop on cosmetic items: dragon hats, tail decorations, and auras. Coins can also be purchased, and purchased Coins do not expire. Two categories sit alongside cosmetics. Dragon toys are optional unlockables that deepen interaction, letting you play fetch or give a dragon a custom perch, and Schell Games states they have no impact on gameplay. Dragon boosts are power-ups that grant an extra match or an extra ability use.

## Is Dragon Grove Pay to Win?

No, according to the official FAQ. Schell Games states that all levels can be completed without power-ups. That is a meaningful design commitment, because it means boosts are convenience rather than gates. The monetized items are cosmetics and optional interaction toys.

If you are deciding how to spend, the honest answer is that the documented shop inventory is cosmetic plus toys, and the documented power-ups are optional. Prices are not published in the sources reviewed here, so any specific Coin cost you see quoted is unverified. You can check current pricing directly in the in-game shop once the game is available.

## Practical Reasoning for New Players

Since level design details are not public, any strategy advice has to be general reasoning from the matching-puzzle premise rather than measured data.

Start by learning the hand gestures before optimizing anything else. Petting, waving, and cradling are interaction verbs, not puzzle inputs, but they build the muscle memory for reaching and grabbing that puzzles depend on. Second, treat boosts as a resource you did not need. Because Schell Games confirms every level is completable without power-ups, a stuck level is a signal to re-examine the board's spatial layers rather than to spend. Third, replay groves for stars if you enjoy the loop, since the official page lists it as a supported activity. Fourth, remember that toys are for bonding, not for advantage.
