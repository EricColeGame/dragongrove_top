If you have searched for Dragon Grove and found dragon breeding charts, Roblox servers, or a survival game about a cursed forest, you have landed in the wrong grove. The Dragon Grove covered here is a mixed reality puzzle game from Schell Games, built as a client project with Meta. According to the official project page at [schellgames.com/portfolio/dragon-grove](https://schellgames.com/portfolio/dragon-grove), it is a cozy, seated spatial puzzler for Meta Quest 3 headsets and up, played entirely with native hand tracking. No controller, no treadmill, no raid group.

This article sticks to what Schell Games has actually published, separates that from reasonable player inference, and flags what is simply not documented yet.

## What Dragon Grove Actually Is

Dragon Grove is a casual puzzle and entertainment title made in Unity for mixed reality. It is free to play and expected in spring 2027. The official description frames it as low-stress: you sit, you use your hands, and you match magical dragon plants to awaken them and grow your grove.

The identity matters because the name is crowded. It is not the Steam game Dragon's Grove from Frozen Flame, and it is not Merge Dragons. It is also not a Roblox experience. If a page you are reading talks about Roblox codes or Steam keys, close the tab.

One thing worth stating clearly: the official FAQ says the art, character assets, and gameplay contain no AI components. If you see a site claiming otherwise, it is not working from developer documentation.

## Advertised Features, Straight From the Official Page

The developer's own feature list is the most reliable thing to plan around. Confirmed advertised features include:

- 200+ levels at launch, spread across 30 unique groves
- 18 dragons at launch, all unlockable through progression, starting with Pip, the signature green dragon
- 3D matching puzzles with new plant types, layers, and spatial mechanics
- Daily streak rewards and magical charms
- Meeting new dragons and tracking friends' journeys
- Replaying groves to earn more stars
- Coins earned by completing levels, spendable in the shop on dragon hats, tail decorations, and auras
- Optional dragon toys like fetch and a custom perch
- Dragon boosts that grant an extra match or ability use

Two details stand out for anyone worried about monetization. Coins can also be purchased and do not expire, but the game is explicitly not pay to win: all levels can be completed without power-ups. Dragon toys are cosmetic and interaction-focused, with no gameplay impact. If a guide tells you a toy is required to progress, that guide is wrong.

## How the Hand Tracking Changes Puzzle Play

Dragon Grove's core interaction is your hands. You pet, wave to, and cradle dragons, and you match leaves and flowers by physically manipulating them. That is a meaningful difference from thumbstick-based matching games, and it shapes how you should think about sessions.

General reasoning from the matching-puzzle premise: when your input is a hand gesture rather than a button press, precision comes from comfortable posture and clear sightlines, not from speed. Mixed reality games reward players who set up a sensible play space and keep their hands inside it. A seated, cozy game is telling you something about intended session length. Long grind sessions are not the point.

Beyond that, the official material does not describe specific puzzle solutions, combo systems, or scoring formulas. Dragon abilities are mentioned as a progression reward, but the developer has not published what each ability does. If a third-party guide lists ability names and cooldowns, treat it as community observation rather than developer documentation, and verify in-game.

## Progression, Coins, and What You Can Verify Yourself

Progression in Dragon Grove appears to run on a straightforward loop described officially: complete levels, earn Coins and stars, unlock dragons, replay groves for more stars, spend Coins in the shop. Twenty-eight of the eighteen launch dragons sit behind progression rather than payment, which is unusual and worth noting plainly.

What is not documented: coin prices, star thresholds for unlocking anything, the full ability list, grove themes, or a detailed content roadmap. Schell Games has said more content will be announced through an official roadmap or patch notes. Until those appear, any site quoting specific prices or unlock requirements is either guessing or scraping something unverified. The reliable check is the in-game shop and the official page, not an aggregator.

The absence of AI-generated assets is another verifiable claim. If you are evaluating the game, that is a concrete, checkable statement from the FAQ rather than a marketing adjective.

## Practical Expectations for a Spring 2027 Launch

If you are deciding whether Dragon Grove is for you, the honest answer depends on hardware and temperament. You need a Meta Quest 3 or newer. You need to be comfortable using bare hands in mixed reality rather than controllers. And you need to want a seated, low-pressure puzzle loop rather than a competitive or combat-driven one.

Third-party guides can be useful for general matching-puzzle habits, like scanning the whole board before your first move or clearing layers strategically. Those habits transfer from other match games as reasoning, not as measured Dragon Grove data. Nobody outside the studio has played the shipping build, so treat any "best strategy" claim as speculation.
