Dragon Grove is a cozy mixed-reality puzzle game from Schell Games, developed as a client project with Meta and built for Meta Quest 3 headsets and up. If you have seen the name floating around and are not sure what it refers to, that is understandable: several unrelated games use similar words, and Dragon Grove is not the Roblox experience, not the Steam title *Dragon's Grove* from Frozen Flame, and not *Merge Dragons*. This article sticks to what Schell Games has actually published about the game, separates that from community impressions, and is honest about what is still unknown.

## What Dragon Grove Actually Is

According to the [official Dragon Grove page on schellgames.com](https://schellgames.com/portfolio/dragon-grove), the project is a casual puzzle and entertainment title made in Unity for Mixed Reality. It is free to play and expected in spring 2027. The studio describes it as seated, cozy, and low-stress, aimed at both family and adult audiences.

The defining technical detail is that you play entirely with native hand tracking on Meta Quest 3 and above, with no controller. You pet dragons, wave to them, cradle them, and match leaves and flowers using your hands. That combination of passthrough mixed reality and hand input is the core of the pitch, not a peripheral feature.

The official FAQ also states that the art, character assets, and gameplay contain no AI components, which is a specific and useful clarification given how often that question comes up around new releases.

## How the Puzzle Loop Likely Works, and Where Advice Ends

Schell Games has confirmed 3D matching puzzles with new plant types, layers, and spatial mechanics. That is the full extent of the documented mechanical description. It has not published specific match rules, combo systems, or scoring formulas.

Because of that, any strategy advice you read online, including here, is reasoning from the matching-puzzle premise rather than measured data. With that caveat stated plainly, a few general habits tend to serve players well in spatial matching games:

- Scan the whole 3D space before your first match. In mixed reality, pieces can sit behind or above what a flat screenshot would show.
- Clear clustered plant types early. Clusters tend to create cascades, and cascades tend to be efficient.
- Save dragon boosts for levels you have already failed once. Since every level is completable without them, using them reactively beats spending them speculatively.
- Replay completed groves for stars rather than grinding new ones if you are short on stars.

None of this is confirmed by the developer, and none of it guarantees success. It is pattern reasoning, nothing more.

## What Is Not Documented

This is where most articles about Dragon Grove go wrong, so it is worth being direct. Schell Games has not published:

- Names of groves beyond the fact that there are 30
- Names of dragons beyond Pip
- Plant names, level names, or star thresholds
- Coin prices, shop item prices, or bundle contents
- Dragon abilities in any specific detail
- Grove themes or biomes
- Patch notes, roadmap specifics, or codes
- Any release date more precise than spring 2027

If you see a guide listing "the best dragon abilities" with named skills and numbers, that content is not sourced from the developer. The honest answer is that dragon abilities are described only as "helpful" and tied to bonding and progression. How they actually function will be visible in the game itself and on the official page as launch approaches.

## Community Impressions Versus Official Information

Third-party guides and community discussion are useful for one thing: they show how players actually experience the game. But they are secondary observations, not developer documentation. Treat them accordingly.

A practical way to sort any claim you encounter:

1. If it appears on the Schell Games Dragon Grove page or official FAQ, treat it as authoritative.
2. If it appears in a third-party guide, treat it as one player's observation, subject to change between builds.
3. If it appears nowhere official and has specific numbers attached, assume it is invented until you can verify it in-game.

Because the game is free to play on Meta Quest 3 and up, verification is cheap: install it and check the shop, the grove list, and the dragon roster yourself. That beats trusting any summary, including this one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Dragon Grove a Roblox game?**
No. It is a mixed-reality game from Schell Games made in collaboration with Meta, built for Meta Quest 3 and above. It is unrelated to any Roblox experience with a similar name.

**Do I need controllers to play?**
No. The game is played entirely with native hand tracking. You use your hands to match plants and to interact with dragons.

**Is it pay to win?**
The studio states it is not, and that all levels can be completed without power-ups. Coins can be bought, but they do not expire, and shop items listed so far are cosmetic.

**When does it come out?**
Spring 2027 is the only window Schell Games has given. No specific date has been published.

**How many dragons are there?**
18 at launch, all unlockable through progression, starting with Pip. No other dragon names have been officially confirmed.

**Does it use AI-generated art?**
The official FAQ says the art, character assets, and gameplay contain no AI components.

For anything beyond this, the [official page](https://schellgames.com/portfolio/dragon-grove) and the studio's FAQ remain the only places worth trusting.
