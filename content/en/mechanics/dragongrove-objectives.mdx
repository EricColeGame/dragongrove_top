## What Dragon Grove Actually Is

Schell Games lists Dragon Grove as a client project with Meta under the genres Casual, Puzzle, and Entertainment, built in Unity for Mixed Reality. The audience is described as family and adult, and the design intent is explicitly cozy: seated, low-stress play you can pick up without a time commitment.

The core loop is a 3D spatial matching puzzle. You match leaves and flowers using your hands, and doing so awakens magical dragon plants. As your grove grows, you unlock helpful dragon abilities and build a bond with the dragons themselves. Petting, waving at, and cradling dragons are all native hand-tracking interactions rather than button prompts.

That detail matters more than it might sound. Because the game runs on hand tracking with no controller fallback, the physicality of matching and dragon interaction is the product, not a gimmick layered on top of a flat puzzle grid. Schell Games advertises the puzzles as having new plant types, layers, and spatial mechanics — meaning the matching happens in three dimensions around you rather than on a single plane.

## Hand Tracking Changes How You Should Think About the Puzzles

General reasoning from the matching-puzzle premise, not measured data: when matching happens in 3D with your hands, the usual advice about speed and combo chains gets less useful. What tends to matter more is spatial awareness — noticing which plants sit at different depths, which ones sit behind others, and where your hands physically are relative to the board.

A few practical habits that follow from that:

- Settle into a comfortable seated position before a session. The game is designed for seated play, and reach distance affects how easily you can address plants on the far side of a grove.
- Move deliberately rather than quickly. Hand tracking rewards clear, readable gestures; snatching at a leaf is more likely to register as an ambiguous motion.
- Use replays. The official page mentions replaying groves to earn more stars, which suggests revisiting earlier content is intended rather than punished.
- Treat dragon boosts as a cushion, not a crutch. Since every level is completable without them, spending them is a comfort decision, not a requirement.

Specific level layouts, star thresholds, and optimal match orders are not documented, and inventing them would be guesswork. If you want that granularity, it will come from playing the game or from community guides written after launch.
