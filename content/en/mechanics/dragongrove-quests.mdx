Dragon Grove is one of the more unusual things headed to a Meta Quest headset: a cozy, seated puzzle game that asks you to match plants with your bare hands while a small dragon watches. It is a client project between Schell Games and Meta, built in Unity for mixed reality, and it is free to play with an expected launch window of spring 2027 on Meta Quest 3 and up.

## What Dragon Grove Actually Is

The official description is short and specific. Dragon Grove is a 3D spatial puzzle game designed exclusively for mixed reality, meaning it uses passthrough to place game objects in your real room rather than dropping you into a fully virtual world. It is casual, family-friendly, and low-stress by design. It is seated play, which is a deliberate choice for a cozy experience rather than a limitation born of the format.

The entire game is played with native hand tracking. There is no controller scheme. You pet dragons, wave at them, cradle them in your hands, and match leaves and flowers by physically grabbing and bringing them together. That is the core loop: match, awaken magical dragon plants, foster the grove.

Your first companion is Pip, the signature green dragon, and everyone starts with Pip. Eighteen dragons total are available at launch, and the official FAQ is clear that all of them are unlockable through progression, not gated behind purchases.

## How the Puzzle Loop Likely Works, and Why It's Not the Same as Merge Dragons

This is where most search confusion happens. Dragon Grove is not Merge Dragons, not the Roblox title, and not the Steam game Dragon's Grove. Searching those names will pull up mechanically unrelated games, so it helps to know what genre this actually is.

Based on the matching-puzzle premise the developer describes, reasonable general reasoning applies. Matching games reward scanning the whole board before acting, prioritizing matches that clear awkward blocked spaces, and holding power-ups rather than spending them on the first tempting opening. Layered and spatial mechanics, which Schell Games advertises, suggest some matches only become available after clearing what sits above them, so order of operations matters more than raw speed. Because the game is explicitly low-stress and seated, there is unlikely to be a timer forcing fast play, which means planning ahead costs nothing.

Treat that as reasoning from the genre, not measured data. Nobody outside the studio has play data yet, and Schell Games has not published level layouts, star thresholds, or coin prices.

## FAQ

**Is Dragon Grove a Roblox game?**
No. It is a standalone mixed-reality title from Schell Games made in collaboration with Meta, exclusive to Meta Quest 3 headsets and up.

**Do I need controllers?**
No. The game is played entirely with native hand tracking.

**Is it pay to win?**
The official FAQ states it is not. All levels can be completed without power-ups, and all 18 launch dragons are unlockable through progression.

**Can I buy Coins?**
Yes. Coins are earned by completing levels and can also be purchased. They do not expire and are used for cosmetics like hats, tail decorations, and auras.

**What are dragon toys and boosts?**
Toys are optional unlockables that expand dragon interaction, for example playing fetch or giving your dragon a perch, with no gameplay impact. Boosts are power-ups that grant an extra match or ability use.

**When does it come out?**
Expected spring 2027. No specific date has been announced.

**Does it use AI-generated art?**
No. The official FAQ states the art, character assets, and gameplay contain no AI components.
