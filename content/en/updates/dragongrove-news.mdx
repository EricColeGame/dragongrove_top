Dragon Grove is easy to mix up with something else. Search the name and you will find a Roblox experience, a Steam survival game with a similar title, and a long-running mobile match-three franchise. None of those are this game. The Dragon Grove covered here is a cozy mixed-reality puzzle title from Schell Games, built with Meta and played on Meta Quest 3 headsets and up using nothing but your hands.

That combination — native hand tracking, seated play, and a low-stress puzzle loop — is unusual enough that most of the useful questions are about how it actually works rather than how hard it gets. The good news is that Schell Games has published a portfolio page and a detailed FAQ, so we can separate what is officially confirmed from what is still unknown. If you want the primary source, the [official Dragon Grove page on Schell Games](https://schellgames.com/portfolio/dragon-grove) is where to start.

## What Dragon Grove Actually Is

Schell Games describes Dragon Grove as a cozy 3D spatial puzzle game designed exclusively for Mixed Reality. It is a client project with Meta, built in Unity, and aimed at both family and adult players. It is free to play and expected in spring 2027.

The important structural detail is that it is Mixed Reality, not fully virtual reality. You are not transported somewhere else. The grove occupies your real room, which is why the game is designed to be played seated and why hand tracking is the only input method. There is no controller. You pet dragons, wave at them, cradle them, and match leaves and flowers with your hands.

The tone is deliberately gentle. Schell Games frames it as cozy and low-stress, which matters for anyone deciding whether this is a game for a child, a parent, or an adult who wants something calm at the end of the day. All three are in the intended audience.

## Gameplay: Matching, Dragons, and Progression

The core loop is a matching puzzle. You match leaves and flowers in 3D space, and doing so awakens magical dragon plants. Over time you build up your grove and bond with the dragons who live there.

At launch the game is advertised to include:

- 200+ levels spread across 30 unique groves
- 18 dragons total, all unlockable through normal progression
- Pip, the signature green dragon, as everyone's starting companion
- Daily streak rewards
- Magical charms
- The ability to track friends' journeys
- New plant types, layers, and spatial mechanics as you advance
- Replayable groves so you can earn more stars

Completing levels earns Coins. Coins are spent in a shop on cosmetic items — dragon hats, tail decorations, and auras. Coins can also be purchased with real money and do not expire.

Two other systems are worth understanding clearly. Dragon toys are optional unlockables that expand interaction: you can play fetch with your dragon or give it a custom perch. Schell Games states these have no impact on gameplay. Dragon boosts are power-ups that grant an extra match or an extra ability use.

### The Monetization Question

Schell Games is explicit that Dragon Grove is not pay to win, and that every level can be completed without power-ups. Coins buy cosmetics and boosts, not progression gates. That is a meaningful distinction for parents evaluating a free-to-play title, though it is worth noting that "not pay to win" is a design claim rather than an independently measured outcome. You can verify how it feels in practice by playing without purchasing anything and seeing whether progression stalls.

## Practical Reasoning About the Puzzles

Because the official materials describe the puzzle premise but not specific solutions, any strategy advice here is general reasoning from how matching puzzles with spatial mechanics tend to work — not measured data from Dragon Grove itself.

Two things follow from the premise. First, matching in three dimensions usually means the constraint is not just "find a pair" but "find a pair you can reach." Layers and spatial mechanics are advertised, which suggests some matches will require the right angle or the right hand position. Slowing down and physically repositioning rather than making fast grabs is likely to be more reliable with hand tracking than treating it like a flat match-three grid.

Second, power-ups that grant an extra match or an extra ability use are typically most valuable when you are stuck near the end of a level rather than early on. Since Schell Games confirms all levels are completable without them, hoarding boosts until you actually need them is a reasonable default.

Star systems and replayable groves generally reward going back to older content with better tools or a clearer understanding. Since replaying groves is confirmed to earn more stars, treating early levels as revisitable rather than disposable is sensible.

None of this guarantees results. It is reasoning from the design, not from playtesting data.

## FAQ

**Is Dragon Grove a Roblox game?**
No. It is a Mixed Reality game by Schell Games for Meta Quest 3 and up, made in collaboration with Meta.

**Do I need controllers?**
No. It is played entirely with native hand tracking. You pet and cradle dragons and match plants with your hands.

**Is it pay to win?**
Schell Games states all levels can be completed without power-ups. Coins buy cosmetics like hats, tail decorations, and auras, plus optional boosts.

**How many dragons are there?**
18 at launch, all unlockable through progression. Everyone starts with Pip.

**Does the game use AI-generated art?**
The official FAQ states the art, character assets, and gameplay contain no AI components.

**When does it come out?**
Spring 2027, free to play, on Meta Quest 3 headsets and up.
