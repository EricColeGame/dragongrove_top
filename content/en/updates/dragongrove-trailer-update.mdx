If you searched for "Dragon Grove" and landed on a Roblox map, a Steam survival title, or a merge puzzle with a similar name, you found the wrong grove. The Dragon Grove this article covers is a mixed-reality game from Schell Games, built as a client project with Meta and advertised for Meta Quest 3 headsets and up. You play it with your bare hands, no controller, seated and low-stress, matching plants and befriending dragons that appear in your actual room.

Here is what is officially documented, what the community can reasonably expect, and what nobody outside the studio actually knows yet.

## What Dragon Grove Actually Is

Per the official Schell Games portfolio page, Dragon Grove is a cozy 3D spatial puzzle game designed exclusively for Mixed Reality. Its genre is listed as Casual, Puzzle, and Entertainment, it runs on Unity, and it is aimed at both family and adult players. It is free to play and expected in spring 2027.

The mixed-reality framing matters more than it sounds. Instead of dropping you into a fully virtual world, the game layers its groves into your physical space through the headset's passthrough cameras. That is a design choice with real consequences for how you play: you are not hiding from your living room, you are gardening inside it.

On the official page, Schell Games advertises daily streak rewards, magical charms, meeting new dragons, tracking friends' journeys, 3D matching puzzles with new plant types and layers, spatial mechanics, and the ability to replay groves to earn more stars. Those are advertised features, not measured outcomes, and the studio has not published a breakdown of how each one works.

## Hands, Not Controllers

Dragon Grove is played entirely with native hand tracking. No controller is required or used. You pet dragons, wave to them, cradle them, and match leaves and flowers with your hands directly.

If you have used hand tracking on Quest before, you know the trade-off. It is intuitive and physical in a way that controllers are not, but it demands decent lighting and a clear play space. For a seated, cozy puzzle game, that trade-off leans in the game's favor. Precision matching with your fingertips is a different skill from pointing a ray, and it is worth calibrating your expectations around that rather than around a traditional VR puzzler.

What is not documented: any gesture list beyond petting, waving, cradling, and matching. If the game ships with pinch shortcuts, palm menus, or gesture-based power-up activation, Schell Games has not said so publicly. You can verify in-game once it launches or watch the official page for updates.

## Dragons, Coins, and What You Can Actually Buy

Everyone starts with Pip, the signature green dragon. Eighteen dragons total are available at launch, and the official FAQ states all of them are unlockable through progression. Completing levels earns Coins, which you spend in the shop on cosmetic items: dragon hats, tail decorations, and auras.

A few points are worth separating cleanly, because they get muddled in speculation:

- Coins can also be purchased, and purchased Coins do not expire.
- Dragon toys are optional unlockables that maximize dragon interaction, letting you play fetch or give your dragon a custom perch. They have no impact on gameplay.
- Dragon boosts are power-ups that grant an extra match or an extra ability use.
- The game is not pay to win. Schell Games states all levels can be completed without power-ups.

| Item type | What it does | Affects gameplay? |
|---|---|---|
| Hats, tail decorations, auras | Cosmetic appearance | No |
| Dragon toys | Extra interaction (fetch, custom perch) | No |
| Dragon boosts | Extra match or ability use | Optional assist |

Prices are not published. Do not trust anyone quoting Coin costs, bundle values, or drop rates before launch.

## Levels, Groves, and Puzzle Reasoning

At launch, Dragon Grove ships with 200+ levels across 30 unique groves. Schell Games says more content will be announced through an official roadmap or patch notes, but those documents do not exist yet as of this writing.

Because the studio has not published grove themes, level names, plant names, or the exact twist each grove introduces, any advice about specific levels is speculation. What can be reasoned from the matching-puzzle premise is general: matching games tend to reward planning a few moves ahead, prioritizing cascades over single matches, and conserving limited-use abilities for boards where you are genuinely stuck rather than spending them early. Layered and spatial mechanics, which Schell Games does advertise, likely mean some matches depend on depth or placement rather than simple adjacency, so scanning the full 3D arrangement before your first move is probably worth the extra second.

Treat all of that as reasoning from genre conventions, not as tested strategy for this game. Dragon abilities are also not documented beyond the existence of boosts. If you want to know what a specific dragon does, the only reliable method is to unlock it and read its in-game description.

## Mixed Reality Setup and Comfort

The game is seated and designed to be low-stress, which is a meaningful accessibility point in a hobby dominated by standing, room-scale experiences. Since it uses passthrough and hand tracking, plan for:

- Adequate room lighting, since hand tracking degrades in dim rooms
- A clear area around your hands and lap
- A seated position where your hands rest comfortably in view of the headset cameras

None of this is official guidance from Schell Games; it is practical inference from how Quest hand tracking behaves. Your mileage will vary by room and headset.

## What Is Not Documented

Schell Games has been unusually clear about one thing: the official FAQ states the art, character assets, and gameplay contain no AI components. Beyond that, a lot of common questions remain open. There is no published roadmap, no patch notes, no confirmed post-launch dragon count beyond the 18 at launch, no pricing for Coins, and no list of dragon abilities. Release timing is spring 2027 and nothing more specific.

If a site claims otherwise right now, it is guessing. Verify against the official page at https://schellgames.com/portfolio/dragon-grove or in the game itself after launch.

## FAQ

**Is Dragon Grove a Roblox game?**
No. It is a mixed-reality game by Schell Games made in collaboration with Meta, for Meta Quest 3 and up.

**Can I play it without controllers?**
Yes. It is played entirely with native hand tracking, by design.

**Is it pay to win?**
No, per the official FAQ. All levels can be completed without power-ups. Coins buy cosmetics, toys, and boosts.

**How many dragons are there?**
Eighteen at launch, all unlockable through progression. Everyone begins with Pip.

**Does it use AI-generated content?**
The official FAQ states the art, character assets, and gameplay contain no AI components.
