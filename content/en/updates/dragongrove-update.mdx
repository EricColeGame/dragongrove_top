If you have searched for "Dragon Grove" and come away confused, you are not alone. The name has been attached to at least three unrelated things: a Roblox experience, a Steam survival game called Dragon's Grove, and a mobile match-and-merge title with a similar name. The one this article covers is different from all of them. Dragon Grove is a cozy 3D spatial puzzle game built exclusively for Mixed Reality by Schell Games in collaboration with Meta, and it is designed to be played with your bare hands on a Meta Quest 3 or newer headset. You can read the developer's own description on the [official Dragon Grove page](https://schellgames.com/portfolio/dragon-grove).

Here is what is actually documented about the game, what the community has noticed, and what nobody knows yet.

## What Dragon Grove Actually Is

Dragon Grove is a client project between Schell Games and Meta, built in Unity, and listed under the Casual, Puzzle, and Entertainment genres for a Family and Adult audience. It is free to play and expected in spring 2027. It runs on Meta Quest 3 headsets and up, meaning older Quest hardware is not part of the stated platform target.

The defining design choice is control. There is no controller in the loop. Every interaction uses native hand tracking: you pet dragons, wave to them, cradle them in your palms, and match leaves and flowers by physically pinching and moving them in your space. Play is seated, low-stress, and described as cozy rather than competitive.

That combination is unusual. Most mixed-reality puzzle games still fall back on controllers, and most cozy games are flat-screen. Dragon Grove sits at the intersection: a seated, hand-tracked spatial puzzler with a dragon-raising wrapper around it.

## The Puzzle Loop and the Dragons Around It

The core activity is matching. You match leaves and flowers with your hands in 3D space, and successful matches awaken magical dragon plants that help your grove grow. The official page advertises 3D matching puzzles with new plant types, layers, and spatial mechanics, which suggests the puzzle board is not a flat grid but something you reach into and rearrange.

General reasoning from the matching-puzzle premise: in games like this, the usual pressure points are limited moves and crowded boards. Because everything is physical rather than cursor-driven, precision matters more than speed. Hand tracking rewards deliberate, clear pinches over fast swipes, so the practical habit is to look at the whole arrangement before you grab anything. That is reasoning, not measured data, and Schell Games has not published a difficulty curve or move economy.

Around the puzzles sits a dragon collection. Everyone begins with Pip, the signature green dragon, and 18 dragons are available at launch. All of them are unlockable through progression, not purchase. Bonding with dragons unlocks helpful abilities, and the official material lists meeting new dragons as a headline feature. Beyond that general statement, the specific abilities each dragon grants are not documented. If you see a guide claiming a particular dragon does a particular thing, treat it as speculation until the game or the official page confirms it.

Levels, when completed, earn Coins. Coins spend in the shop on dragon hats, tail decorations, and auras, and they can also be purchased directly. Bought Coins do not expire.

## How Progression, Stars, and Daily Play Fit Together

Progression appears to work on two tracks. The first is level completion, which pays out Coins. The second is stars, since the official page mentions replaying groves to earn more stars. Higher star counts typically gate content in this genre, but Schell Games has not published star thresholds, so any number you see quoted elsewhere is invented.

Daily streak rewards and magical charms are both advertised features. Streaks are a retention mechanic: log in on consecutive days, collect escalating rewards. Charms are described as magical but their exact function is not spelled out on the official pages, so the honest answer is that we know they exist and not much more.

There is also a social layer. The official feature list includes tracking friends' journeys, which implies some way of seeing another player's progress through the 30 groves.

## What Is Not Pay to Win

This is one of the clearer points in the official FAQ. The game is explicitly not pay to win, and all levels can be completed without power-ups. Dragon boosts are power-ups that grant an extra match or an extra ability use, so they smooth over mistakes rather than unlock anything exclusive. Coins buy cosmetic hats, tail decorations, and auras.

Dragon toys sit in a middle category. They are optional unlockables that maximize dragon interaction, letting you play fetch or give a dragon a custom perch. The developer states these have no impact on gameplay. If you are the kind of player who wants the mechanical edge, toys do not provide one, and if you are the kind who wants the cozy interaction, they do.

## Where Dragon Grove Sits in the Mixed-Reality Puzzle Space

Mixed reality on Quest has mostly produced two things: short tech demos and room-scale action games. A seated, hand-tracked, cozy puzzler with 200+ levels is a different bet. It also fits Schell Games' stated interest in approachable, family-friendly VR, and it fits Meta's push to make hand tracking the default input on Quest 3.

Two things are worth flagging plainly.

First, the official FAQ states that the art, character assets, and gameplay contain no AI components. That is a direct statement from the developer, and it matters to players who care about how assets are produced.

Second, the roadmap. Schell Games says more content will be announced through an official roadmap or patch notes. That means anything beyond 200+ levels, 30 groves, and 18 dragons is undocumented territory. There are no published grove names beyond those mentioned, no plant names, no codes, no hotkeys, and no release date finer than spring 2027.

The reliable way to verify anything you read elsewhere is simple: open the game when it launches and check the in-game shop, star counts, and dragon roster, or read the official FAQ and portfolio page. Third-party guides are useful for general strategy, but they are secondary observations, not developer documentation, and this genre attracts a lot of confident guessing.

## FAQ

**Is Dragon Grove a Roblox game?**
No. The Dragon Grove from Schell Games is a standalone mixed-reality title for Meta Quest 3 and up. Roblox games with similar names are unrelated.

**Is it the same as Dragon's Grove on Steam?**
No. That is a separate survival game from a different developer.

**Do I need controllers?**
No. The game is played entirely with native hand tracking.

**How many dragons are there?**
Eighteen at launch, starting with Pip the green dragon, all unlockable through progression.

**Is it pay to win?**
The developer says no, and that all levels can be completed without power-ups. Coins buy cosmetics, and purchased Coins do not expire.

**When does it come out?**
Spring 2027, free to play. Anything more specific has not been announced.
